## Claims supported

- The material "remains compliant during normal interaction but rapidly stiffens under impact". Cited in [[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]] §2.6.3.
- In a drop test of a 5 kg payload from 1 m "Following the European CE certification protocol", "A 6 mm SRM layer reduced peak impact force by 70.0% from 86.8 kN to 25.8 kN, while a 12 mm layer achieved an 87.1%". Cited in §2.6.3, §2.6.6.
- "Conventional materials, especially thick rubber or Silica Gel, require roughly twice the thickness". Cited in §2.6.3.
- On the ENGINEAI PM01 (1.38 m, 42 kg with battery, 22 actuated joints), the protector "allows repeated falls without hardware damage, including drops from 3 m". Cited in §2.6.3, §2.6.6.

## Test conditions

- **Platform or configuration:** ENGINEAI PM01 humanoid; material samples on a drop rig.
- **Units and measurement boundary:** Contact force from a 0 to 100 kN force sensor sampled at 25 kHz.
- **Operating conditions:** Drop test 5 kg from 1 m; robot falls in several scenarios.
- **Duration, sample size, denominator:** Simulation trials: 400 pushing, 400 shutdown, 400 collision, 100 slipping; number of hardware falls not transcribed.

## Limitations

- Preprint. Long-term durability of the padding and its added mass were not transcribed.
