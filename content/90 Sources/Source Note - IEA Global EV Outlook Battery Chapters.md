## Claims supported

- 2025: "The energy density of LFP battery packs is about one-fifth lower by mass (Wh/kg)" and "about one-third lower by volume (Wh/L) than that of NMC packs". Cited in [[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]] §2.1.3.
- 2025: "LFP's ability to reach 100% state of charge when required without significant degradation"; "NMC batteries are typically limited to 80% to preserve long-term performance". Cited in [[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]] §2.1.2 and §2.1.3.
- 2025: NMC's energy density remains an advantage for "operation in cold climates, where LFP technology is typically less effective". Cited in [[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]] §2.1.3.
- 2026: "In 2025, LFP battery packs were more than 40% cheaper on average than lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide (NMC) alternatives per kWh", with the caveat that "part of this cost advantage reflects the lower energy density requirements of stationary storage applications". Cited in [[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]] §2.1.3.
- Not cited: the 2025 edition also gives LFP as "almost 30% cheaper per kilowatt-hour (kWh)" than NMC, an earlier-year figure.

## Test conditions

- **Platform or configuration:** Electric-vehicle and stationary-storage battery packs, not robot packs.
- **Units and measurement boundary:** Pack level (Wh/kg, Wh/L, cost per kWh).
- **Operating conditions:** Market averages.
- **Duration, sample size, denominator:** Annual market data.

## Limitations

- Vehicle packs are far larger than a robot's; ratios carry over better than absolute values.
- The pages do not give a verified LFP-versus-NMC comparison of thermal safety or cycle life, so 2.1 does not quantify those.
- Only the first part of each page could be checked word for word; the quotes above come from that part.
