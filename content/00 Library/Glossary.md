[[index|Home]]

Terms in alphabetical order. Each entry links to the chapter where the term is first introduced.

- **Annualized cost:** A one-off purchase spread over its useful life, plus yearly running costs (integration, energy, maintenance), so machines with different lifetimes can be compared per year. ([[1.3 - Why Build a Humanoid|1.3]], §1.3.3)
- **Attempt:** One accepted task order, from acceptance until verified success, abort, or hand-off to a human. Retries within the order belong to the same attempt. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.6)
- **Autonomous mobile robot (AMR):** A wheeled robot that navigates a facility on its own, often used to move bins or carts. A common simpler alternative to a humanoid. ([[1.3 - Why Build a Humanoid|1.3]], §1.3.4)
- **Back-EMF:** The voltage a spinning motor generates, proportional to speed; it limits top speed for a given bus voltage. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Backdrivability:** How easily external force can turn a joint, and its motor, back through the gearing. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.3)
- **Backlash:** Free play between gear teeth; when torque reverses, the output does not move until the play is taken up. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.2)
- **Ball screw:** A threaded shaft and nut with rolling balls between them, turning rotation into linear motion efficiently; a roller screw uses threaded rollers instead and carries higher loads. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.3)
- **Bias (sensor):** A constant offset in a sensor's readings. Averaging does not remove it; calibration does. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **Battery management system (BMS):** Electronics and firmware that monitor cell voltages, current, and temperature and keep the pack within safe limits. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.1)
- **Body shop:** The area of a car plant where sheet-metal panels are joined, mostly by welding, into the car body. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.6)
- **Brake resistor (braking chopper):** A resistor switched across the power bus whenever regenerated energy pushes the voltage past a threshold, turning that energy into heat. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Brownout:** A supply voltage dip deep enough to make electronics misbehave or reset, without a full power loss. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.5)
- **Bus (power bus):** The shared supply conductors that carry power from the battery to many loads. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.1)
- **C-rate:** Current expressed relative to a battery's capacity: 1C would empty it in one hour, 2C in half an hour. High C-rates heat and age cells faster. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Calendar aging:** Loss of battery capacity over time even without use, faster at high temperature and high state of charge. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **CAN FD:** Controller Area Network with flexible data rate: classic CAN extended with a faster data phase and messages of up to 64 bytes. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Cell balancing:** Equalizing the charge of cells in a series string, because the weakest cell limits the whole string. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Centre of mass (CoM):** The mass-weighted average position of a body. Carrying a payload shifts the combined robot-plus-payload CoM. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.4)
- **Centre of pressure (CoP):** The point on a contact surface where the resultant ground reaction force acts. For a foot it must stay inside the foot's support area. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.4)
- **Cobot (collaborative robot):** A robot arm designed to work near people, typically with force and speed limits. ([[1.1 - Introduction - From Watts to Work|1.1]], §1.1.3)
- **Conduction loss:** Power lost as current flows through a switch that is on, $I^2 R_{\text{DS(on)}}$. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Continuous torque:** The torque a motor can produce indefinitely without its windings passing their temperature limit, under stated cooling. Compare peak torque. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Contactor:** A heavy-duty electrically controlled switch that connects or isolates a high-current circuit, such as the battery pack. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.1)
- **Cost of transport (specific):** Energy used per unit weight per unit distance, $c = E/(m g d) = P/(m g v)$. Dimensionless; lower is more efficient. Energetic ($c_{\text{et}}$) uses total energy drawn; mechanical ($c_{\text{mt}}$) uses joint work only. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.3)
- **Coulomb counting:** Estimating state of charge by integrating current in and out of the battery; accurate short-term, but drifts. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Current loop:** The innermost motor controller, which sets motor current (and so torque) thousands of times per second. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **Cycle aging:** Loss of battery capacity from charging and discharging, faster with deep cycles, high C-rates, and heat. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Cycloidal drive:** A gearbox in which an eccentric lobed disc rolls inside a ring of pins, giving a moderate to high ratio in one stage with low backlash. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.3)
- **DC/DC converter:** Power electronics that turn one DC voltage into another, for example the main bus into the low, regulated voltages compute and sensors need. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Dead time:** A short pause between turning off one switch of an inverter leg and turning on the other, so the two never conduct at once and short the bus. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Derating:** Lowering the torque, current or speed a joint is allowed as it nears its temperature limit, so it weakens early instead of shutting down. ([[2.8 - Thermal Management and Energy Budgets|2.8]], §2.8.2)
- **Degree of freedom (DoF):** One independent way a body can move; for a robot, usually one actuated joint. Placing a rigid body at an arbitrary pose in 3D takes six. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.2)
- **Dynamically stable:** Stays upright only through active control, such as a biped keeping its centre of mass over a small support area. Contrast with statically stable. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.3)
- **Effective mass:** The mass a point on a limb, such as the hand, feels like when it hits something, including the reflected inertia of the motors behind it. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.4)
- **Electromagnetic compatibility (EMC):** Keeping the electrical noise a device emits, and its sensitivity to noise from others, low enough that nearby circuits work correctly. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Electronic fuse (eFuse):** A transistor switch with current sensing that limits or cuts current within microseconds, can be reset, and reports faults. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Encoder:** A sensor that measures angle or position, optically, magnetically, or with a resolver. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **End-of-arm tooling:** A gripper or tool built for one part or part family, as used on fixed industrial robots. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.3)
- **Energy (Wh):** An amount of work or stored charge; power accumulated over time. $1\ \text{Wh} = 3600\ \text{J}$. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.2)
- **Energy density (Wh/L):** Energy stored per unit volume. Compare specific energy. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Error budget:** A split of an allowed total error (such as placement tolerance) among its independent sources, so each subsystem gets its own limit. Independent errors combine as a root sum of squares. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.5)
- **Drift (sensor):** Bias that changes slowly with time or temperature, so it must be re-estimated while the robot runs. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **EtherCAT:** An industrial Ethernet bus in which one frame passes through every device each cycle, each device reading and writing its data on the fly; devices share clocks to within 100 ns. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Evidence ladder:** The ordered stages a robot claim can reach: simulation, demo, customer pilot, paid deployment, repeat deployment. Each stage shows something the earlier ones do not. ([[1.1 - Introduction - From Watts to Work|1.1]], §1.1.3)
- **Extrinsic calibration:** Finding where a sensor sits, in position and orientation, relative to the robot's body. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **Field-oriented control (FOC):** Motor control that splits the phase currents into a torque-producing part and a non-torque part using the rotor angle, and regulates each, giving smooth torque proportional to current. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Fixture (jig):** A rigid holder that locates and clamps parts in an exact position, for example for welding. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.6)
- **Floating base:** A robot body not fixed to the world, such as a humanoid's pelvis. Its six position and orientation variables have no motor of their own and are moved only through contact forces. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.2)
- **Form closure:** Holding an object by geometry, such as hooking under or around it, so friction is not needed. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Gallium nitride (GaN) transistor:** A power switch that turns on and off faster than a silicon MOSFET, with lower switching loss. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.3)
- **Gate driver:** The small circuit that switches an inverter's power transistors on and off. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Gear ratio ($N$):** How many motor turns make one joint turn; it multiplies torque and divides speed. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.2)
- **Hand-eye calibration:** Finding where a camera is relative to the robot's hand or base, so a point the camera sees can be reached. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **Harmonic drive (strain-wave gear):** A gearbox in which a flexible toothed cup, squeezed into an oval, meshes with a rigid ring; high ratio in one stage with near-zero backlash. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.3)
- **Humanoid robot (working definition):** A mobile robot with a person-like body plan: torso, two arms with end effectors, sensing mounted high, and two legs or, in some designs, a wheeled base, sized to work in spaces built for people. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.1)
- **Impedance control:** Commanding how stiff and damped a joint or hand should behave, like a spring and shock absorber, instead of an exact position; lets the robot yield on contact. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.4)
- **Inertial measurement unit (IMU):** A sensor package of accelerometers and gyroscopes that measures a body's acceleration and rotation rate. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.1)
- **Intrinsic calibration:** Finding a sensor's own parameters, such as a camera's focal length, optical centre and lens distortion. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **Inrush current:** The surge drawn when discharged capacitors are first connected to a supply, limited only by the small resistance in the path. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Internal resistance:** A cell's resistance to current; it makes voltage sag under load and turns current into heat, and rises in the cold and with age. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Intervention:** Any human action needed to start, continue, rescue, reset, or finish an attempt, including remote teleoperation and approval prompts. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.5)
- **Jitter:** Variation in timing from one cycle to the next, such as a loop that usually runs every 1.000 ms but sometimes at 1.2 ms. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.1)
- **Joint module:** Motor, drive, gearing, encoders, output bearing and seals packaged as one unit with one power and communication connection. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.2)
- **KV rating:** A motor's no-load speed per volt of supply, in rpm per volt; used mostly for hobby and drone motors. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **IP code (ingress protection):** The IEC 60529 rating of an enclosure: a first numeral for solids, 0 to 6 (no ingress of dust), and a second for liquids, 0 to 9. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.2)
- **Latency:** Delay from a measurement or event to the response that uses it. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **Lithium iron phosphate (LFP):** A lithium-ion chemistry with lower specific energy than nickel-based cells but lower cost and a wider usable range. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.3)
- **Load-carrying shell:** An outer skin that is itself the structure, as in a car body, instead of covers over an internal frame. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.3)
- **Middleware:** The messaging layer that lets separate robot software processes exchange data with defined delivery behaviour. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **Mobile manipulator:** A wheeled mobile base carrying one or more robot arms; a common simpler alternative to a humanoid on flat floors. ([[1.3 - Why Build a Humanoid|1.3]], §1.3.4)
- **Motor constant ($K_m$):** Torque per square root of watt of copper loss, in N m/√W; a higher value means more torque for the same heat. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Motor drive (inverter):** Power electronics that turn the battery's direct current into the controlled current each motor needs. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.1)
- **Nickel-based lithium-ion (NMC, NCA):** Lithium-ion chemistries with nickel-rich cathodes (nickel manganese cobalt, nickel cobalt aluminum); higher specific energy than LFP. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.3)
- **Operating domain:** The defined set of conditions (objects, places, floor, environment, people, workflow, duty cycle) under which a robot's requirements must hold. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.3)
- **Opportunity charging:** Topping up a smaller battery during breaks or idle time instead of carrying a full shift of energy. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.3)
- **Opposition (pad, palm, side):** The direction in which a hand squeezes an object: between fingertips, against the palm, or from the side. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Peak torque:** The torque a motor can produce briefly, limited by drive current, magnet saturation, and how fast the windings heat. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Permanent-magnet synchronous motor (PMSM):** A brushless motor with magnets on the rotor, driven with sinusoidal currents; the usual humanoid joint motor. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Planetary gearbox:** A gearbox in which a central sun gear drives planet gears rolling inside a ring gear; low to moderate ratio per stage, and stages can be stacked. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.3)
- **Pose:** Position plus orientation of an object or body. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.6)
- **Power (W):** The rate of energy transfer. $1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}$. Peak power sizes drives, wiring, and battery current ratings. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.2)
- **Power grasp:** A grasp that holds the object rigidly against the palm, so the arm must make every movement of the object. Compare precision grasp. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Pre-charge:** Charging a bus's capacitors slowly, usually through a resistor, before the main switch closes, to avoid inrush current. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Precision grasp:** A fingertip grasp that lets the hand move the object without moving the arm. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Precision Time Protocol (PTP, IEEE 1588):** A network protocol that synchronizes the clocks of measurement and control devices, typically to well under a microsecond. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.2)
- **Protective stop:** A safety function that stops motion when a hazardous condition is detected. It is separate from, and must not depend on, task software. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.5)
- **Pulse-width modulation (PWM):** Switching a voltage fully on and off very fast, with the on-share of each cycle setting the average voltage. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Quasi-direct drive (QDD):** An actuator with a large motor and a low gear ratio, so it stays backdrivable and can sense force from motor current (proprioceptive actuation). ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.3)
- **Rare-earth elements (REEs):** The group of metals, including neodymium and dysprosium, used in the strongest permanent magnets. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.7)
- **Real time (hard real time):** Bounded timing: every answer arrives before its deadline, every time. A system can be fast without being real time. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Real-time operating system (RTOS):** An operating system that runs the most urgent ready task first and keeps the delay before it runs bounded. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Redundant degree of freedom:** A joint beyond the six needed to place a hand or foot, such as a seventh arm joint; it lets the limb change shape while the end effector stays put. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.2)
- **Reflected inertia:** The motor rotor's inertia as felt at the joint, $N^2 J_{\text{rotor}}$; it grows with the square of the gear ratio. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.2)
- **Regeneration:** Energy a motor returns to its supply when it is slowed down or lowers a load, acting as a generator. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Robots-as-a-service (RaaS):** A commercial model where the customer pays a recurring fee for robots or their output instead of buying them outright. ([[1.1 - Introduction - From Watts to Work|1.1]], §1.1.3)
- **Sacrificial part:** A cheap part, such as a cover or bumper, designed to break in a fall or collision and be replaced, protecting the costlier parts behind it. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.3)
- **Safe torque off (STO):** A drive safety function that prevents torque-producing power from reaching the motor, independently of control software; an uncontrolled stop (stop category 0). ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Safety extra-low voltage (SELV):** A circuit kept at a voltage low enough, and separated well enough from higher voltages, that touching it is not considered dangerous under normal conditions. The limits are set in IEC standards. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.3)
- **Second moment of area ($I$):** A property of a section's shape, in m⁴, that sets its bending stiffness; material far from the centre adds most, which is why links are hollow. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.2)
- **Selectivity:** Arranging protection so only the device nearest a fault acts, leaving the rest of the system powered. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.2)
- **Sensorless control:** Motor control that estimates rotor angle from voltages and currents instead of an encoder; weak at very low speed and standstill. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.3)
- **Series elastic actuator (SEA):** An actuator with a spring between gearbox and load; the spring's deflection measures force and protects the gearbox from shocks. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.3)
- **Service loop:** Slack cable left outside a joint so it can flex as the joint moves. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.3)
- **Setpoint:** The target value a controller drives toward, such as a joint angle or torque. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **Shunt:** A small, precise resistor in a current path; the voltage across it measures the current. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Slip ring:** Sliding contacts that pass power or signals across a joint that rotates without limit. ([[2.2 - Power Distribution and Protection|2.2]], §2.2.3)
- **Special-purpose (dedicated) automation:** Equipment built for one task, such as a conveyor, sorter, or fixed robot cell; usually faster and cheaper per operation than a general-purpose robot on that task. ([[1.3 - Why Build a Humanoid|1.3]], §1.3.2)
- **Specific energy (Wh/kg):** Energy stored per unit mass; lower for a whole pack than for its cells. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Specific stiffness:** Young's modulus divided by density; nearly the same, about 25 MN m/kg, for steel, aluminum and magnesium. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.2)
- **Standard test conditions (STC):** The rating conditions for solar modules: 1000 W/m2 irradiance, AM1.5 spectrum, 25 °C cell temperature. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.4)
- **State estimation:** Combining sensor readings into a best estimate of the robot's own position, orientation, and velocity, and of the objects around it. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **State of charge (SoC):** How full a battery is, as a percentage; estimated, not measured directly. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **State of health (SoH):** How much of a battery's original capacity remains. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.2)
- **Statically stable:** Stays upright with motors off because its centre of mass sits inside a wide support area, as with a wheeled base. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.3)
- **Stop category 0:** An uncontrolled stop by immediate removal of power to the actuators, as defined in IEC 60204-1 (cited through TI). ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Structured light:** A depth sensor that projects a known light pattern and triangulates how the scene distorts it. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.3)
- **Switching loss:** Power lost in a switch during each turn-on and turn-off, growing with voltage, current, transition time and switching frequency. ([[2.3 - Motor Drives and Power Electronics|2.3]], §2.3.2)
- **Tactile sensor:** A touch sensor in a fingertip or palm that reports contact location, pressure and sometimes shear, used to detect slip. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Takt time:** The pace a production line must run at to meet demand: available time divided by required units. Every station must finish within it. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.8)
- **Teleoperation:** Human control of a robot through a remote or supervisory interface. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.5)
- **Tendon drive:** Motors in the forearm pulling cables through the wrist to move the fingers, keeping the hand light. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.2)
- **Thermal capacitance:** Heat a part absorbs per degree of temperature rise, in J/°C. Larger means it heats more slowly. ([[2.8 - Thermal Management and Energy Budgets|2.8]], §2.8.2)
- **Thermal resistance:** Temperature rise per watt of heat flowing along a path, in °C/W. Smaller means a better heat path. ([[2.8 - Thermal Management and Energy Budgets|2.8]], §2.8.2)
- **Thermal runaway:** A self-accelerating overheating of a cell that can spread to neighbouring cells and cause fire. ([[2.1 - Battery Packs Battery Management and Charging|2.1]], §2.1.5)
- **Thermal soak test:** Running the robot on its real duty cycle, at the site's maximum ambient, until temperatures settle, to check every part stays below its limit. ([[2.8 - Thermal Management and Energy Budgets|2.8]], §2.8.5)
- **Thermal time constant:** Thermal resistance times thermal capacitance; the time to reach 63% of the final temperature rise. ([[2.8 - Thermal Management and Energy Budgets|2.8]], §2.8.2)
- **Time-of-flight camera:** A depth sensor that times light pulses returning to each pixel. ([[2.9 - Sensors Calibration and Time Synchronization|2.9]], §2.9.3)
- **Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN):** IEEE 802.1 standards that give switched Ethernet bounded latency, low delay variation and low loss. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Tool changer:** A coupling at the wrist that lets a robot drop one gripper or tool and pick up another. ([[2.7 - Hands Grippers and End Effectors|2.7]], §2.7.3)
- **Torque constant ($k_t$):** Motor torque per amp of torque-producing current, in N m/A; its value depends on the current convention used. ([[2.4 - Motors and Actuator Selection|2.4]], §2.4.2)
- **Torsional stiffness:** How little a transmission twists under torque; a soft transmission lets the output lag and ring after fast moves. ([[2.5 - Transmissions and Joint Modules|2.5]], §2.5.2)
- **Tote:** A reusable plastic bin used to store and move goods in warehouses. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.6)
- **Traceability:** Linking each requirement to its parent (why it exists) and to how it is verified, so a change shows everything downstream that must be re-checked. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.7)
- **Transmission:** A mechanism (gearbox, belt, screw) that trades motor speed for joint torque. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.1)
- **Underactuated:** Having fewer actuators than configuration variables. A humanoid's floating base is unactuated, so it is controlled indirectly through contacts. ([[1.2 - What Is a Humanoid Robot|1.2]], §1.2.2)
- **Useful work:** A customer task completed to the customer's definition of done, verified, safely, repeatedly at a measured rate, and at a competitive cost per successful task. ([[1.1 - Introduction - From Watts to Work|1.1]], §1.1.3)
- **Verification:** Confirming a system meets its specified requirements, by test, analysis, inspection, or demonstration. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.7)
- **Warehouse management system (WMS):** The software that tracks inventory and assigns tasks in a warehouse; it sends work to robots and records results. ([[1.5 - From Use Case to Engineering Requirements|1.5]], §1.5.8)
- **Watchdog:** A timer that must be reset every cycle; if the code that resets it is late or stuck, it fires and triggers a safe response. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Whole-body control:** A controller that computes all joint torques together to track task goals while respecting dynamics, contact limits, and balance. ([[1.4 - How a Humanoid Works - The Full System Map|1.4]], §1.4.3)
- **Worst-case execution time (WCET):** The longest a piece of code can take on given hardware; real-time deadlines must hold against it, not against the average. ([[2.10 - Embedded Compute and Real-Time Communications|2.10]], §2.10.2)
- **Young's modulus ($E$):** A material's resistance to stretching, in Pa; with the section's second moment of area it sets how much a link bends. ([[2.6 - Mechanical Architecture and Structures|2.6]], §2.6.2)
