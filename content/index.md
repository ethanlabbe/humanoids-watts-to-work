## Purpose

This book is an engineering information bank built around one question:

> What has to happen between drawing power from a battery and completing a customer's task safely, repeatedly, and at a competitive cost?

It is written for engineers of any discipline: it assumes comfort with maths, physics, and units, but no particular specialty. Specialist terms from every field, whether controls, perception, electronics, mechanical, manufacturing, or commercial, are defined on first use and collected in the [[Glossary]]. The architecture is hardware-agnostic; named platforms appear only as dated case studies. Each use case is compared against simpler alternatives such as conveyors, mobile robots with fixed arms, or people.

Two recurring cases run through the chapters, both introduced in [[1.1 - Introduction - From Watts to Work|1.1]]:

- **Tote transfer (the spine):** pick up a warehouse tote, carry it, place it, verify. Used in every chapter.
- **Fixture loading (the contrast):** place a sheet-metal part into a welding fixture on a car body-shop line. Used where the tote is too forgiving: hands, precise perception, contact-rich manipulation, learning, and safety (2.7, 3.4, 3.8, 3.10, 5.3).

Evidence rules:

- Durable engineering principles are kept separate from dated industry snapshots.
- Claims are labelled as vendor-reported, customer-reported, independently measured, illustrative estimate, or unknown. Missing evidence is Unknown, never zero.
- Evidence stages are kept distinct: simulation, demo, customer pilot, paid deployment, repeated production deployment.
- Links sit next to the claims they support.
